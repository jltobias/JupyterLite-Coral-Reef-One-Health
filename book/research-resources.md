# Research tools and next steps

[Awesome coral research](https://github.com/luismmontilla/awesome-coral-research), maintained by GitHub user **luismmontilla and contributors**, is a discovery directory covering datasets, monitoring, image annotation, microbiology, protocols, and other research topics. It helped identify the external resources below. Their descriptions were checked against the providers' own websites on **2 November 2026**.

## Connect tools to research questions

| Question | External resource | A possible learning activity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What environmental heat stress might a reef experience? | [NOAA Coral Reef Watch](https://coralreefwatch.noaa.gov/satellite/index.php): satellite sea-surface temperature, heat-stress, and bleaching-alert products | Compare a dated heat-stress product with habitat coverage from the mapping lesson; record their different spatial and temporal scales |
| How could field observations complement satellite habitat maps? | [MERMAID](https://datamermaid.org/): reef survey data collection, exploration, and management | Identify the sampling methods, observation dates, and access conditions needed to compare benthic and fish surveys |
| How can reef photographs become observations? | [CoralNet](https://coralnet.ucsd.edu/): image organization, annotation, and annotation statistics | Design a small annotation exercise and explain how label definitions and annotator agreement could affect results |
| Which marine organisms have been recorded near a study area? | [Ocean Biodiversity Information System (OBIS)](https://obis.org/): marine biodiversity data discovery | Examine observation dates, coordinates, taxonomic names, and sampling effort before interpreting occurrence records |

These links open separate services. They are not installed in this repository's JupyterLite environment, and the starter notebooks do not call their APIs or download their datasets.

## Plan a defensible extension

Choose one question from the [One Health lesson](one-health.md). Write down the outcome you want to understand, which observations could measure it, and what a map can contribute. Then choose an appropriate external resource and record its data version, units, observation period, permissions, and requested citation.

Before comparing observations, check their spatial extent, observation dates, class definitions, and survey effort. A heat-stress alert is not an observation of bleaching at every reef; image labels need validation; occurrence records need sampling context. Any connection to community health requires additional evidence and locally agreed research practices.

Use the bundled notebooks to practice documenting assumptions before replacing their teaching inputs. A future real-data notebook should describe provenance, missing values, uncertainty, and how a reader can reproduce the analysis.
